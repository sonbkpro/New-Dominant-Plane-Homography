# Calibrated Dominant-Plane Consensus for Trustworthy Unsupervised Deep Homography Estimation
# Revised plan (planv1) — one-direction prediction, EM-grounded posterior, pruned loss set.

## 0. Changelog Relative To plan.txt

1. Switched from bidirectional (\(H_{ab}, H_{ba}\)) to one-direction prediction (\(H_{ab}\) only).
   The inverse mapping \(H_{ab}^{-1}\) is obtained in closed form from the predicted matrix and
   used only inside losses, not as a second prediction head.
2. Replaced the bidirectional inverse-consistency loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}\) with a
   feature-space cycle loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cycle}}\) computed using the analytic inverse.
3. Grounded the dominant-plane posterior \(q_i\) in an explicit two-component generative
   residual model and trained it with an EM-style loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}\).
   This converts \(q_i\) from "renamed attention mask" to amortized variational inference.
4. Dropped \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}}\): it duplicates the uncertainty term inside
   \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\) (Kendall-Gal likelihood) and risks fighting it.
5. Dropped \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}\): the current CA-Homography pair list does not
   expose ordered triplets, so temporal composition is not implementable without a new sampler.
6. Restricted invalid-pair generation to intra-batch random shuffle and patch reshuffle.
   Cross-video and large-temporal-gap pairs are removed because video-id metadata is unavailable.
7. Added a hard-negative mining channel for the reliability head to prevent it from
   collapsing onto trivial "content mismatch" cues.
8. Specified the cross-image feature interaction: a correlation block whose output feeds
   the posterior and uncertainty heads.

Net result: 5 essential losses + 1 optional (cycle), down from 8 in plan.txt.

## 1. Central Thesis

Reformulate unsupervised deep homography estimation as a probabilistic dominant-plane
consensus problem. Given two real images \(I_a\) and \(I_b\), the method jointly estimates:

\[
\bigl( H_{ab},\; q_{ab}(i),\; \sigma_{ab}(i),\; s_{ab} \bigr),
\]

where

1. \(H_{ab}\) is the dominant-plane homography from \(I_a\) to \(I_b\).
2. \(q_{ab}(i) = P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b)\) is the posterior that pixel \(i\) belongs to
   the dominant-plane support, derived from a two-component generative residual model.
3. \(\sigma_{ab}(i)\) is the spatial aleatoric uncertainty.
4. \(s_{ab} \in [0,1]\) is the pair-level reliability score: the probability that the
   image pair admits a reliable single dominant homography.

The paper is framed as "trustworthy" homography estimation, not as "yet another mask or
backbone." The model answers three questions for every pair:

1. What is the best dominant homography?
2. Where is this homography valid?
3. Should this homography be trusted?

## 2. Motivation From The Existing Repository

The repository implements Oneline-DLT (file: Oneline-DLTv1/resnet.py):

1. ResNet-34 backbone consumes the channel-stacked patch pair and regresses an
   8-dimensional four-corner offset \(\Delta p_{ab}\).
2. A single-channel feature extractor (`ShareFeature`) is applied to each patch.
3. A sigmoid mask predictor (`genMask`) produces an attention mask reused as loss
   weighting.
4. The training loss is the masked feature-space triplet:
   \[
   \mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
   = \frac{\sum_i M_i \max(0,\; 1 + d_i^+ - d_i^-)}{\sum_i M_i},
   \]
   with \(d_i^+ = \lVert F_b(i) - F_a'(i) \rVert_1\) and
   \(d_i^- = \lVert F_b(i) - F_a(i) \rVert_1\).

What is missing relative to a TIP-level proposal:

1. The mask is not a probabilistic posterior over a defined latent.
2. There is no notion of spatial uncertainty.
3. There is no notion of pair-level reliability.
4. There is no calibration metric or failure-detection protocol.
5. There is no anti-collapse or support regularization on the mask beyond `normMask`.
6. There is no cross-image correspondence module: the two patches are simply
   channel-concatenated before the ResNet.

These are the gaps the revised plan fills.

## 3. Novelty Positioning

The contribution is positioned along two axes:

1. **Probabilistic reformulation**: \(q_i\) is the posterior of a two-component
   generative residual model; the network is the amortized recognition function;
   training uses an EM-style alignment loss. This is qualitatively different from
   any prior content-aware mask (Zhang et al. ECCV 2020), dynamic mask (Le et al.),
   or pseudo-plane mask (Mask-Homo).
2. **Trustworthiness evaluation protocol** for homography estimation: failure
   detection (AUROC, AUPRC), calibration (ECE, NLL), and risk-coverage curves
   driven by \(s_{ab}\). To the best of our knowledge, no prior deep-homography
   paper reports this protocol.

The paper does NOT claim novelty in backbones, attention mechanisms,
multi-homography decomposition, transformer matching, or foundation-model
distillation. It also does NOT claim novelty in aleatoric uncertainty as a
technique; the novelty is its integration with dominant-plane consensus and a
calibrated reliability head, evaluated under a new trustworthiness protocol.

## 4. Literature Boundaries

The proposal explicitly distinguishes itself from:

1. Supervised deep homography (DeTone et al.).
2. Photometric unsupervised homography (Nguyen et al.).
3. Content-aware mask learning (Zhang et al., ECCV 2020).
4. Dynamic-scene masks (Le et al.).
5. Motion-basis homography (BasesHomo).
6. Transformer / recurrent refinement (RHWF).
7. Geometrized transformer matching (GeoFormer).
8. Multi-homography plane masks (Mask-Homo).
9. Adaptive query masking (AGNet).
10. Foundation-model feature teaching (SAMFeat).

## 5. Proposed Method

### 5.1 Inputs And Outputs

Input: \((I_a, I_b)\), small-baseline real image pair.

Outputs:

\[
H_{ab},\quad
q_{ab}(i) = P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b),\quad
\log \sigma_{ab}(i),\quad
s_{ab} \in [0,1].
\]

\(H_{ab}^{-1}\) is computed in closed form when needed for the cycle loss; it is
not a separate network output.

### 5.2 Feature Extractor

A lightweight multi-scale CNN (ResNet-18 / 34 backbone reused from the existing
repository) produces shared features \(F_a^{(l)}, F_b^{(l)}\) at two scales
\(l \in \{1/4, 1/8\}\). The 1/4 features feed alignment losses; the 1/8 features
feed the cross-image correspondence module.

### 5.3 Cross-Image Correspondence Module (new)

A compact correlation block computes local cost volumes:

\[
C(i, d) = \langle F_a^{(1/8)}(i),\; F_b^{(1/8)}(i + d) \rangle, \quad d \in [-R, R]^2,
\]

with a small radius \(R\) (e.g. \(R = 4\)). The cost volume is reduced via a few
conv layers to a per-pixel feature \(c_i\) used as input to:

1. The dominant-plane posterior head \(q_i\).
2. The spatial uncertainty head \(\log \sigma_i\).

This module is what makes \(q_i\) and \(\sigma_i\) actually depend on
inter-image correspondence quality, rather than only on single-image content.

### 5.4 Homography Estimator (unchanged interface)

Keep the four-corner offset representation and the differentiable DLT solver
(`DLT_solve` in utils.py):

\[
\Delta p_{ab} \in \mathbb{R}^{8},\quad
p' = p + \Delta p_{ab},\quad
H_{ab} = \mathrm{DLT}(p, p').
\]

The 8-DoF regression head takes the concatenation of \([F_a, F_b]\) features
(as in the current code) plus the pooled correlation feature.

### 5.5 Generative Residual Model And EM-Style Posterior

This is the principal novelty.

Define the photometric / feature residual after warping:

\[
r_i = \lVert F_b(i) - W(F_a, H_{ab})(i) \rVert_1.
\]

Posit a two-component mixture model over residuals:

1. Inlier (dominant-plane consistent): \(p(r_i \mid z_i = 1) \propto \exp(-r_i / \sigma_i)\)
   (Laplacian with predicted scale).
2. Outlier (non-planar, dynamic, occluded, low-texture):
   \(p(r_i \mid z_i = 0) = 1 / r_{\max}\), a broad uniform on \([0, r_{\max}]\).
   \(r_{\max}\) is a constant chosen from data statistics.

With a learned prior \(\pi_i = P(z_i = 1)\) produced by the network, the
posterior in the E-step is

\[
\tilde q_i =
\frac{\pi_i\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 1)}
     {\pi_i\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 1) + (1 - \pi_i)\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 0)}.
\]

The network's amortized posterior head outputs \(q_i\). It is trained to match
the stop-gradient E-step posterior \(\tilde q_i\) (loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}\),
Section 6.3). The M-step is implicit: gradient descent on \(H\) under the
\(q_i\)-weighted alignment loss.

This grounding is what distinguishes the work from a content-aware mask: \(q_i\)
is the amortized posterior of a defined generative model, and convergence
arguments follow the standard variational EM template.

### 5.6 Spatial Uncertainty

The uncertainty head outputs \(\log \sigma_i\). It is trained jointly with
\(H_{ab}\) via the Kendall-Gal heteroscedastic likelihood inside
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\); no separate calibration loss is used.

### 5.7 Pair-Level Reliability

The reliability head consumes pooled statistics:

\[
\phi_{ab}
=
\bigl[
\bar{q},\; \mathrm{var}(q),\; |\{i : q_i > \tau\}| / N,
\;
\bar{r},\; \overline{q \cdot r},\; \overline{\sigma},
\;
\bar{c}_{\text{cycle}}
\bigr],
\]

where \(\bar{c}_{\text{cycle}}\) is the mean feature residual of the analytic
cycle check
\(\big\lVert F_a - W\bigl(W(F_a, H_{ab}), H_{ab}^{-1}\bigr) \big\rVert_1\).

No bidirectional consistency term appears in \(\phi_{ab}\), because the network
predicts only one homography.

\(s_{ab} = \mathrm{MLP}(\phi_{ab})\).

## 6. Loss Functions (revised — 5 essential, 1 optional)

The total loss is

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
+ \lambda_a \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}
+ \lambda_e \mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}
+ \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_{\text{support}}
+ \lambda_v \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}
+ \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}
+ \lambda_c \mathcal{L}_{\text{cycle}}.
\]

The classical bidirectional \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}\), the redundant
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}}\), and the data-incompatible
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}\) are removed.

### 6.1 Consensus-Weighted Triplet (essential, anti-collapse)

\[
d_i^+ = \lVert F_b(i) - W(F_a, H_{ab})(i) \rVert_1,\quad
d_i^- = \lVert F_b(i) - F_a(i) \rVert_1.
\]

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
=
\frac{\sum_i q_i \max(0,\; m + d_i^+ - d_i^-)}{\sum_i q_i + \epsilon},
\quad m = 1.
\]

This loss is kept because its negative term \(d_i^-\) is the only mechanism
preventing the feature extractor from collapsing to a constant map. The mask
is replaced by the consensus posterior \(q_i\).

### 6.2 Uncertainty-Weighted Alignment (essential)

Charbonnier penalty \(\rho(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + \epsilon^2}\) and Kendall-Gal
heteroscedastic likelihood:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}
=
\frac{\sum_i q_i \bigl[ \rho(r_i / \sigma_i) + \log \sigma_i \bigr]}
     {\sum_i q_i + \epsilon}.
\]

This single loss does two jobs: it trains \(H_{ab}\) under a robust
likelihood and it trains \(\log \sigma_i\) via the \(\log \sigma\) penalty.
A separate calibration loss is therefore unnecessary and is removed.

### 6.3 EM Posterior Loss (essential — grounds the posterior claim)

Compute the E-step target

\[
\tilde q_i =
\frac{\pi_i\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 1)}
     {\pi_i\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 1) + (1 - \pi_i)\, p(r_i \mid z_i = 0)},
\]

with stop-gradient on \(r_i\), \(\sigma_i\), and \(\pi_i\). Train the network's
posterior \(q_i\) to match \(\tilde q_i\) via binary cross entropy:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}
=
\frac{1}{N} \sum_i
\Bigl[ -\tilde q_i \log q_i - (1 - \tilde q_i) \log(1 - q_i) \Bigr].
\]

This is the substitute for any ad hoc "make q look like the residual" term.
\(\pi_i\) may simply be set to a constant prior (e.g. 0.5) for simplicity;
ablate constant vs. learned prior.

### 6.4 Support Regularization (essential — prevents trivial degeneracy)

Floor the average posterior to avoid collapse:

\[
\bar q = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i q_i,\quad
\mathcal{L}_{\text{support}} = \max(0,\; \alpha - \bar q)^2,
\]

with \(\alpha = 0.10\) as the default. With \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}\) in
play this term can be weighted smaller than in plan.txt, but it is still
needed because in early training residuals are uninformative and the E-step
can drive \(q\) toward zero.

### 6.5 Edge-Aware Smoothness Of Consensus (small weight)

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}
=
\sum_i \bigl(|\partial_x q_i| + |\partial_y q_i|\bigr)
\exp\bigl(-\gamma |\nabla I_b(i)|\bigr).
\]

### 6.6 Reliability Loss With Hardened Negatives (essential)

Without video-id metadata, invalid pairs are generated by:

1. Intra-batch shuffle: pair \(I_a\) from sample \(k\) with \(I_b\) from sample \(k'\).
2. Patch reshuffle: extract two non-overlapping patches from the same image and
   pair them as if they were different images.
3. **Hard-negative mining (added)**: at each iteration, treat in-batch pairs
   whose current point-residual proxy (mean of \(r_i\) over the patch) is above
   a running percentile threshold as soft negatives, with target label \(0.5\)
   rather than \(0\) or \(1\). This mitigates the shortcut where the head
   learns to detect "any image-content mismatch" rather than "geometric
   infeasibility."

Loss:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}
=
-\bigl[\, y_{ab} \log s_{ab} + (1 - y_{ab}) \log(1 - s_{ab}) \,\bigr].
\]

\(y_{ab} = 1\) for the natural pair, \(y_{ab} = 0\) for shuffled and patch-
reshuffle pairs, \(y_{ab} = 0.5\) for hard-mined pairs.

### 6.7 Analytic-Inverse Cycle (optional, recommended)

Use the closed-form inverse of the predicted \(H_{ab}\):

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{cycle}}
=
\frac{1}{N} \sum_i
\Bigl\lVert F_a(i) - W\bigl(W(F_a, H_{ab}),\; H_{ab}^{-1}\bigr)(i) \Bigr\rVert_1.
\]

No second prediction head is introduced. This replaces \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}\)
from plan.txt and provides geometric regularization within the strict
one-direction prediction setting.

### 6.8 Initial Weights (to be ablated)

\[
\lambda_a = 1.0,\;
\lambda_e = 0.5,\;
\lambda_s = 0.01,\;
\lambda_v = 0.001,\;
\lambda_r = 0.1,\;
\lambda_c = 0.05.
\]

The unweighted term is \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}\) (coefficient 1).

### 6.9 Why The Removed Losses Are Removed

1. \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} = \lVert H_{ab} H_{ba} - I \rVert_F^2\): requires
   bidirectional prediction; removed by design choice. Replaced by
   \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cycle}}\).
2. \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}} = |\log \sigma_i - \log r_i|\) (stop-grad):
   redundant with the \(\log \sigma\) term in \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\)
   under the Kendall-Gal likelihood. Keeping both creates two conflicting
   signals on \(\sigma_i\) and risks collapsing \(\sigma_i\) onto \(r_i\) (the
   exact failure mode the original "Risk 3" tried to avoid).
3. \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}\): requires same-video frame triplets. The
   current `Train_List.txt` is a list of pairs and the dataset loader does
   not expose video-id or frame order. Implementing this requires a new
   metadata file; deferred to future work.

## 7. Architecture Plan

### 7.1 Base Network

Reuse the existing model as the skeleton:

1. Keep `DLT_solve` and `transform` from utils.py.
2. Keep `ShareFeature` as the single-scale feature extractor (extend to a
   1/4 + 1/8 multi-scale variant).
3. Replace `genMask` with a posterior head that consumes \([F_a^{(1/4)}, F_b'^{(1/4)}, c]\),
   where \(F_b'\) is \(F_b\) warped to \(I_a\)'s patch coordinates with the
   identity initialization, and \(c\) is the reduced correlation feature.
   Critically, \(q\) depends on the current \(H_{ab}\): re-evaluate after
   the homography head fires (one inner iteration is sufficient).

### 7.2 New Heads

1. Homography head \(g_H(F_a, F_b, c) \to \Delta p_{ab}\).
2. Posterior head \(g_q(F_a, F_b, c, r) \to q_{ab} \in [0,1]\).
3. Uncertainty head \(g_\sigma(F_a, F_b, c) \to \log \sigma_{ab}\).
4. Reliability head \(g_s(\phi_{ab}) \to s_{ab} \in [0,1]\).

### 7.3 Forward Output Dictionary

```
{
    "H_ab": H_ab,
    "H_ab_inv": H_ab_inv,                 # closed-form, not a learned output
    "offset_ab": offset_ab,
    "q_ab": q_ab,                          # posterior P(z=1 | I_a, I_b)
    "log_sigma_ab": log_sigma_ab,
    "reliability_score": s_ab,
    "residual_map_ab": r_ab,
    "feature_a": F_a, "feature_b": F_b,
    "loss_triplet": L_triplet,
    "loss_align":   L_align,
    "loss_em":      L_em,
    "loss_support": L_support,
    "loss_smooth":  L_smooth,
    "loss_rel":     L_rel,
    "loss_cycle":   L_cycle,
    "loss_total":   L_total
}
```

## 8. Dataset Plan

### 8.1 Existing Dataset

Use CA-Homography (RE / LT / LL / SF / LF). Manual coordinate labels are kept
only for evaluation. No new annotation is required for training.

### 8.2 Pair Sampling

1. Natural pair \((I_a, I_b)\): from `Train_List.txt` as in the current loader.
2. Intra-batch shuffle for invalid pairs: at each iteration, draw a permutation
   \(\pi\) and form auxiliary pairs \((I_a^{(k)}, I_b^{(\pi(k))})\) with target
   \(y = 0\).
3. Patch reshuffle for invalid pairs: from a single image, extract two
   non-overlapping patches with the same crop geometry and present them as a
   pair with target \(y = 0\).
4. Hard-negative mining: within natural pairs, flag the highest-residual
   fraction (e.g. top 10 %) as label-smoothed negatives \(y = 0.5\).

Temporal triplets and cross-video pairing are not used (no metadata).

## 9. Evaluation Plan

### 9.1 Accuracy Metrics (geometric)

Per-pair reprojection error from the manual point correspondences:

\[
e_j = \lVert \pi(H_{ab}\, x_j^a) - x_j^b \rVert_2,\quad
E = \frac{1}{K} \sum_j e_j.
\]

Report mean and median \(E\); AUC at 1, 3, 5 pixels; inlier ratio at 1, 3, 5
pixels; category-wise results (RE, LT, LL, SF, LF) plus average.

### 9.2 Trustworthiness Metrics (the methodological contribution)

Define a pair as a "failure" if \(E > \tau\), with \(\tau \in \{3, 5\}\) px.
Report:

1. AUROC for failure detection driven by \(1 - s_{ab}\).
2. AUPRC for failure detection.
3. Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of \(s_{ab}\).
4. Negative log-likelihood of the failure label under \(s_{ab}\).
5. Risk-coverage curves: sort by \(s_{ab}\), reject the lowest fraction \(c\),
   report mean \(E\) on the kept set.
6. Reliability diagrams.

### 9.3 Qualitative

For each scene category and for selected invalid pairs: input pair, warped
result, \(q_{ab}\) heatmap, \(\sigma_{ab}\) heatmap, \(s_{ab}\) scalar,
point-reprojection visualization.

## 11. Ablation Studies

Essential ablations (each is a binary toggle relative to the full model unless
noted):

1. Base Oneline-DLT (sigmoid mask, triplet only) — sanity baseline.
2. \(+\) consensus posterior \(q_i\) trained only by \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}\)
   (no EM, no align).
3. \(+\) Kendall-Gal alignment loss (uncertainty added).
4. \(+\) EM posterior loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}\).
5. \(+\) support regularization.
6. \(+\) edge-aware smoothness.
7. \(+\) reliability head and \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}\).
8. \(+\) cycle loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cycle}}\).
9. Posterior prior \(\pi_i\): constant \(0.5\) vs. learned head.
10. Cross-image correspondence module on vs. off.
11. Single-scale vs. multi-scale features.
12. Reliability training without hard-negative mining vs. with.

The single most important ablation: "sigmoid attention mask + triplet only"
(item 1) vs. "consensus posterior + EM + Kendall-Gal" (items 2 + 3 + 4 stacked).
This isolates the conceptual contribution and is the cleanest defense against
the "renamed mask" objection.

## 12. Claimed Contributions

1. A two-component generative residual model for unsupervised deep homography
   estimation, with an amortized posterior \(q_i\) trained by an EM-style loss.
2. A unified probabilistic framework producing \((H, q, \sigma, s)\) with a
   single one-direction prediction head, in which inverse-related regularization
   is recovered via the closed-form inverse of the predicted homography.
3. A pruned, justified loss design: five essential losses plus one optional
   cycle loss, removing redundancy that appears in prior multi-loss formulations.
4. A reliability head trained with shuffled and patch-reshuffled negatives plus
   in-batch hard-negative mining, defended against the "content-mismatch
   shortcut" failure mode.
5. A trustworthiness evaluation protocol for homography estimation (AUROC,
   AUPRC, ECE, NLL, risk-coverage, reliability diagrams) and improved accuracy
   and reliability across the five CA-Homography scene categories.

## 14. Risks And Mitigations

1. **"Posterior is just a mask" objection.**
   Mitigation: ground \(q_i\) in the two-component generative residual model,
   show the EM target \(\tilde q_i\), and report the ablation that turns
   \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{em}}\) off.

2. **Reliability head shortcuts.**
   Mitigation: include hard-negative mining and report ablation row 12 to
   demonstrate the contribution of the mined negatives.

3. **Uncertainty copies residuals.**
   Mitigation: the Kendall-Gal likelihood (and the absence of a separate
   stop-gradient calibration loss) makes "copying" the residual non-trivial,
   because the model pays for it in the alignment term. Verify via reliability
   diagrams.

4. **Closed-form \(H_{ab}^{-1}\) numerical issues.**
   Mitigation: condition-number guard at each step; skip cycle loss on
   ill-conditioned matrices; the term is optional anyway.

5. **Non-reproducible recent baselines.**
   Mitigation: clearly report code/weights/format availability; no fabricated
   numbers.

## 16. Final Recommendation

The original plan was strong but carried three structural issues for a TIP
submission: a bidirectional commitment that the user did not want, a loss
suite with two redundant terms, and a "posterior" claim that was not
mathematically grounded.

The revised plan addresses all three:

1. Strict one-direction prediction with an analytic-inverse cycle loss.
2. Pruned loss set: 5 essential + 1 optional, with explicit justification for
   every retained term and every removed term.
3. EM/variational grounding for \(q_i\) under a two-component residual model.

The transformation is from

\[
(I_a, I_b) \to H_{ab}
\]

to

\[
(I_a, I_b) \to \bigl( H_{ab},\; P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b),\; \sigma_i,\; s_{ab} \bigr),
\]

framed as amortized variational inference over dominant-plane consensus and
evaluated under a trustworthiness protocol that the homography subfield does
not yet have.
