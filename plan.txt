# Calibrated Dominant-Plane Consensus for Trustworthy Unsupervised Deep Homography Estimation

## 1. Central Thesis

This project should not be positioned as another deep homography network with a better backbone, mask, or attention module. The stronger IEEE TIP-level idea is to reformulate homography estimation as a problem of calibrated dominant-plane consensus:

Given two real images \(I_a\) and \(I_b\), the method estimates not only a homography \(H_{ab}\), but also:

\[
P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b),
\]

where \(z_i = 1\) means pixel or region \(i\) belongs to the dominant plane-induced homography, and a calibrated reliability score:

\[
s_{ab} = P(\text{single homography is valid} \mid I_a, I_b).
\]

The key claim is:

Existing deep homography methods force every image pair into a single homography, even when the single-plane assumption is violated by parallax, dynamic objects, large foregrounds, or weak texture. This work estimates the homography together with its geometric validity domain and calibrated trustworthiness.

The paper should emphasize trustworthy homography estimation, not only lower average reprojection error.

## 2. Motivation From The Current Code And Paper

The attached ECCV 2020 paper, "Content-Aware Unsupervised Deep Homography Estimation", introduced three important ideas:

1. A learned feature extractor \(f(\cdot)\) for feature-space alignment.
2. A learned content-aware mask \(m(\cdot)\) for outlier suppression.
3. A triplet-style unsupervised loss to avoid trivial feature collapse.

The current repository implements the simplified Oneline-DLT version:

1. It predicts one forward homography \(H_{ab}\).
2. It uses a one-channel learned feature map.
3. It uses a sigmoid mask as attention and loss weighting.
4. It uses a simplified hinge/triplet feature loss:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
= \sum_i M_i \max(0, 1 + d_i^+ - d_i^-),
\]

where:

\[
d_i^+ = \left|F_b(i) - F_a'(i)\right|,
\]

\[
d_i^- = \left|F_b(i) - F_a(i)\right|,
\]

and \(F_a'\) is the feature of \(I_a\) after warping by the predicted homography.

However, this version does not fully model:

1. Bidirectional inverse consistency.
2. Whether the mask is a true geometric consensus probability.
3. Whether the homography is reliable.
4. Whether uncertainty is calibrated.
5. Whether the single-homography assumption is valid.

These are the main opportunities for a novel paper.

## 3. Novelty Positioning

The proposed method should be framed as:

Calibrated Dominant-Plane Consensus Homography Estimation.

The central novelty is not "adding uncertainty" or "adding temporal consistency". The novelty is a probabilistic formulation in which homography estimation produces:

1. A dominant-plane homography \(H_{ab}\).
2. A posterior support map \(q_i = P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b)\).
3. A spatial uncertainty map \(\sigma_i\).
4. A scalar reliability score \(s_{ab}\).
5. A failure-detection mechanism for cases where a single homography is physically invalid.

This makes the model answer three questions:

1. What is the best dominant homography?
2. Where is this homography valid?
3. Should this homography be trusted?

This is more defensible for IEEE TIP than a module-combination paper.

## 4. Literature Boundaries To Avoid Duplication

The proposal must explicitly distinguish itself from the following known directions:

1. Supervised deep homography estimation:
   DeTone et al. estimate homography from synthetically warped image pairs.

2. Unsupervised photometric homography:
   Nguyen et al. minimize photometric error using differentiable warping.

3. Content-aware mask learning:
   Zhang et al. ECCV 2020 already introduced learned features and content-aware masks.

4. Dynamic-scene masks:
   Le et al. studied deep homography estimation with dynamic object masks.

5. Motion-basis and low-rank homography:
   BasesHomo models homography flow with motion bases and subspace projection.

6. Transformer and recurrent refinement:
   RHWF and related methods use recurrent homography-guided warping and attention.

7. Geometrized transformer matching:
   GeoFormer uses RANSAC-guided focused attention for self-supervised homography.

8. Multi-homography plane masks:
   Mask-Homo estimates pseudo-plane masks and multiple homographies.

9. Adaptive query masking:
   AGNet addresses query over-focusing in transformer homography estimation.

10. Foundation-model feature teaching:
    SAMFeat uses SAM to guide local feature learning for homography estimation.

The proposed paper should not claim novelty in masks, transformers, multi-homography estimation, or SAM-guided features. Its novelty should be:

\[
\text{homography} + \text{dominant-plane posterior} + \text{calibrated uncertainty} + \text{validity/failure prediction}.
\]

## 5. Proposed Method

### 5.1 Inputs And Outputs

Input:

\[
(I_a, I_b)
\]

where \(I_a\) and \(I_b\) are two small-baseline real images or video frames.

Outputs:

\[
H_{ab},
\]

\[
H_{ba},
\]

\[
q_{ab}(i) = P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b),
\]

\[
\log \sigma_{ab}(i),
\]

\[
s_{ab} \in [0, 1].
\]

Here:

1. \(H_{ab}\) maps image \(I_a\) to image \(I_b\).
2. \(H_{ba}\) maps image \(I_b\) to image \(I_a\).
3. \(q_{ab}(i)\) is the dominant-plane posterior.
4. \(\sigma_{ab}(i)\) is the predicted spatial uncertainty.
5. \(s_{ab}\) is the pair-level reliability score.

### 5.2 Feature Extractor

Use a lightweight multi-scale feature extractor:

\[
F_a^l = f_l(I_a),
\]

\[
F_b^l = f_l(I_b),
\]

where \(l\) indexes feature scales.

For a single-GPU implementation, use a compact CNN or ResNet18-style backbone rather than a large transformer. The paper should avoid relying on a huge backbone as the source of novelty.

### 5.3 Homography Estimator

Retain the DLT-compatible four-corner offset representation used by the current code:

\[
\Delta p_{ab} \in \mathbb{R}^{8}.
\]

Given source patch corners:

\[
p = \{p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4\},
\]

the destination corners are:

\[
p' = p + \Delta p_{ab}.
\]

The homography is obtained by differentiable DLT:

\[
H_{ab} = \text{DLT}(p, p').
\]

Predict both directions:

\[
H_{ab} = g(F_a, F_b),
\]

\[
H_{ba} = g(F_b, F_a).
\]

### 5.4 Dominant-Plane Posterior

Replace the ordinary attention mask \(M_i\) with a probabilistic latent variable:

\[
z_i \in \{0, 1\}.
\]

The network predicts:

\[
q_i = P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b).
\]

The interpretation is:

1. \(q_i \approx 1\): region \(i\) is consistent with the dominant plane-induced homography.
2. \(q_i \approx 0\): region \(i\) is likely dynamic, non-planar, textureless, occluded, or geometrically unreliable.

This must be described as a geometric consensus posterior, not an attention mask.

### 5.5 Spatial Uncertainty

The model predicts:

\[
\log \sigma_i.
\]

The uncertainty should explain feature residuals:

\[
r_i = \left\|F_b(i) - W(F_a, H_{ab})(i)\right\|_1.
\]

Here \(W(\cdot, H)\) denotes differentiable warping by homography \(H\).

High \(\sigma_i\) should correspond to regions where alignment is inherently uncertain.

### 5.6 Reliability Score

The model predicts:

\[
s_{ab} = P(y_{ab}=1 \mid I_a, I_b),
\]

where \(y_{ab}=1\) means the image pair admits a reliable single dominant homography.

The reliability head should use pooled statistics from:

1. The dominant-plane posterior \(q_i\).
2. The residual map \(r_i\).
3. The uncertainty map \(\sigma_i\).
4. The homography feature embedding.
5. Optional inverse-consistency residuals.

A possible reliability feature vector is:

\[
\phi_{ab}
=
\left[
\text{mean}(q),
\text{var}(q),
\text{area}(q > \tau),
\text{mean}(r),
\text{mean}(q r),
\text{mean}(\sigma),
\left\|H_{ab}H_{ba} - I\right\|_F
\right].
\]

Then:

\[
s_{ab} = \text{MLP}(\phi_{ab}).
\]

## 6. Loss Functions

### 6.1 Robust Uncertainty-Weighted Alignment Loss

Use a robust residual:

\[
r_i = \left\|F_b(i) - W(F_a, H_{ab})(i)\right\|_1.
\]

Use a Charbonnier penalty:

\[
\rho(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + \epsilon^2}.
\]

The uncertainty-weighted dominant-plane loss is:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}^{ab}
=
\frac{
\sum_i q_i
\left[
\rho\left(\frac{r_i}{\sigma_i}\right)
+ \log \sigma_i
\right]
}{
\sum_i q_i + \epsilon
}.
\]

The bidirectional version is:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}^{ab}
+
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}^{ba}.
\]

### 6.2 Consensus-Weighted Triplet Loss

Keep the useful idea from the original paper, but reinterpret the weighting as consensus probability:

\[
d_i^+ = \left\|F_b(i) - W(F_a, H_{ab})(i)\right\|_1,
\]

\[
d_i^- = \left\|F_b(i) - F_a(i)\right\|_1.
\]

Then:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}^{ab}
=
\frac{
\sum_i q_i \max(0, m + d_i^+ - d_i^-)
}{
\sum_i q_i + \epsilon
}.
\]

Use \(m=1\) initially, matching the current implementation.

### 6.3 Inverse Consistency Loss

Enforce:

\[
H_{ab}H_{ba} \approx I.
\]

Loss:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}
=
\left\|H_{ab}H_{ba} - I\right\|_F^2.
\]

This restores an important part of the original paper that the simplified Oneline version does not fully use.

### 6.4 Support Regularization

The posterior \(q_i\) can collapse to a tiny set of easy pixels. Prevent this with a soft support constraint.

Let:

\[
\bar{q} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i q_i.
\]

Require a minimum non-degenerate support \(\alpha\):

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{support}}
=
\max(0, \alpha - \bar{q})^2.
\]

Use \(\alpha = 0.05\) or \(\alpha = 0.10\) initially.

To avoid forcing dense masks, do not require \(\bar{q}\) to be large. The goal is only to prevent collapse.

### 6.5 Spatial Smoothness Of Consensus

Encourage locally coherent validity maps:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}
=
\sum_i
\left(
\left|\partial_x q_i\right|
+
\left|\partial_y q_i\right|
\right)
\exp(-\gamma \left|\nabla I_b(i)\right|).
\]

This allows discontinuities near image edges.

### 6.6 Uncertainty Calibration Loss

The predicted uncertainty should correlate with true residual magnitude. A simple self-supervised calibration target is:

\[
\hat{\sigma}_i = \text{stopgrad}(r_i).
\]

Use:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\left|
\log \sigma_i
-
\log(\hat{\sigma}_i + \epsilon)
\right|.
\]

This term should be low-weighted to avoid making uncertainty merely copy residuals.

### 6.7 Reliability Loss With Synthetic Invalid Pairs

Create invalid pairs without manual labels:

1. Pair frames from different videos.
2. Pair frames with very large temporal distance.
3. Pair image patches with inconsistent crops.

For valid pairs:

\[
y_{ab}=1.
\]

For invalid generated pairs:

\[
y_{ab}=0.
\]

Use binary cross entropy:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}
=
-
y_{ab}\log s_{ab}
-
(1-y_{ab})\log(1-s_{ab}).
\]

This gives the model a direct training signal for rejecting impossible or unreliable homographies.

### 6.8 Temporal Composition Consistency

If video triplets are available:

\[
(I_t, I_{t+1}, I_{t+2}),
\]

then:

\[
H_{t,t+2}
\approx
H_{t+1,t+2}H_{t,t+1}.
\]

Loss:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}
=
\left\|
H_{t,t+2}
-
H_{t+1,t+2}H_{t,t+1}
\right\|_F^2.
\]

Use this only when the dataset loader can safely sample frame triplets from the same video.

### 6.9 Total Loss

The full objective is:

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}
+
\lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
+
\lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}}
+
\lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{support}}
+
\lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}
+
\lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}}
+
\lambda_6 \mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}
+
\lambda_7 \mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}.
\]

Initial default weights:

\[
\lambda_1 = 1.0,
\quad
\lambda_2 = 0.01,
\quad
\lambda_3 = 0.01,
\quad
\lambda_4 = 0.001,
\quad
\lambda_5 = 0.05,
\quad
\lambda_6 = 0.1,
\quad
\lambda_7 = 0.1.
\]

These should be tuned by ablation, not treated as final.

## 7. Architecture Plan

### 7.1 Base Network

Start from the existing Oneline-DLT model:

1. Keep differentiable DLT.
2. Keep patch-based training.
3. Keep feature-space alignment.
4. Keep the current training pipeline as the baseline.

### 7.2 New Model Heads

Add the following heads:

1. Homography head:

\[
\Delta p_{ab} = g_H(F_a, F_b).
\]

2. Consensus posterior head:

\[
q_{ab} = \sigma(g_q(F_a, F_b, r)).
\]

3. Uncertainty head:

\[
\log \sigma_{ab} = g_\sigma(F_a, F_b, r).
\]

4. Reliability head:

\[
s_{ab} = g_s(\phi_{ab}).
\]

### 7.3 Suggested Output Dictionary

The forward pass should return:

```
{
    "H_ab": H_ab,
    "H_ba": H_ba,
    "offset_ab": offset_ab,
    "offset_ba": offset_ba,
    "validity_prob_ab": q_ab,
    "validity_prob_ba": q_ba,
    "log_sigma_ab": log_sigma_ab,
    "log_sigma_ba": log_sigma_ba,
    "reliability_score": s_ab,
    "residual_map_ab": r_ab,
    "residual_map_ba": r_ba,
    "loss_align": loss_align,
    "loss_triplet": loss_triplet,
    "loss_inv": loss_inv,
    "loss_support": loss_support,
    "loss_smooth": loss_smooth,
    "loss_calib": loss_calib,
    "loss_rel": loss_rel,
    "loss_temp": loss_temp,
    "loss_total": loss_total
}
```

## 9. Evaluation Plan

### 9.1 Accuracy Metrics

Use the manually provided point correspondences for evaluation:

\[
e_j =
\left\|
\pi(H_{ab} x_j^a) - x_j^b
\right\|_2.
\]

Mean reprojection error:

\[
E =
\frac{1}{K}
\sum_{j=1}^{K} e_j.
\]

Report:

1. Mean reprojection error.
2. Median reprojection error.
3. AUC at 1, 3, and 5 pixels.
4. Inlier ratio at 1, 3, and 5 pixels.
5. Category-wise results for RE, LT, LL, SF, and LF.

### 9.2 Trustworthiness Metrics

Define failure by a threshold:

\[
y_{\text{fail}} = \mathbb{1}[E > \tau].
\]

Use \(\tau = 3\) pixels and \(\tau = 5\) pixels.

Evaluate:

1. AUROC for failure detection.
2. AUPRC for failure detection.
3. Expected calibration error.
4. Negative log likelihood.
5. Risk-coverage curves.
6. Reliability diagrams.

Risk-coverage is especially important:

Sort predictions by reliability \(s_{ab}\). Reject the lowest-confidence predictions. Measure whether reprojection error decreases as coverage decreases.

This demonstrates that the reliability score is useful, not cosmetic.

### 9.3 Qualitative Results

Show:

1. Input pair.
2. Warped result.
3. Dominant-plane posterior \(q_i\).
4. Uncertainty map \(\sigma_i\).
5. Failure/reliability score \(s_{ab}\).
6. Point reprojection visualization.

Important qualitative cases:

1. Moving foreground.
2. Large foreground.
3. Low texture.
4. Low light.
5. Strong parallax.
6. Invalid pairs.

## 12. Expected Contributions For The Paper

The paper should claim the following contributions:

1. A probabilistic dominant-plane consensus formulation for unsupervised deep homography estimation.
2. A joint prediction framework for homography, geometric validity posterior, spatial uncertainty, and pair-level reliability.
3. A self-supervised training objective using robust feature likelihood, inverse consistency, support regularization, calibration, synthetic invalid pairs, and optional temporal composition.
4. A trustworthiness evaluation protocol for homography estimation, including failure detection, calibration, and risk-coverage analysis.
5. Improved accuracy and reliability on regular, low-texture, low-light, small-foreground, and large-foreground scenes.

## 14. Risks And Mitigations

### Risk 1: Reviewers see the method as a mask variant

Mitigation:

Do not call \(q_i\) an attention mask. Define it as a latent dominant-plane posterior and evaluate calibration/failure detection.

### Risk 2: Reliability score learns shortcuts

Mitigation:

Use multiple invalid-pair generation strategies and evaluate on real failure cases defined by point reprojection error.

### Risk 3: Uncertainty map copies residuals

Mitigation:

Use stop-gradient targets only as a weak calibration term. The main training should be through likelihood.

### Risk 4: Temporal consistency is unavailable

Mitigation:

Make temporal consistency optional. The core novelty does not depend on it.

### Risk 5: Recent baselines are hard to reproduce

Mitigation:

Use all runnable official implementations. For non-runnable methods, cite them clearly and explain the reproducibility limitation.

## 16. Final Recommendation

The previous plan was too close to a module-combination story. This revised plan is stronger because it changes the problem formulation:

From:

\[
(I_a, I_b) \rightarrow H_{ab}
\]

to:

\[
(I_a, I_b)
\rightarrow
\left(
H_{ab},
P(z_i = 1 \mid I_a, I_b),
\sigma_i,
s_{ab}
\right).
\]

This makes the work about reliable geometric estimation under violated planar assumptions. That is a much better IEEE TIP narrative than simply improving the content-aware homography network.
